# Talk Structure

## Working shape

This web deck should not feel like a linear product demo. It should feel like a
small investigation that slowly turns into engineering.

1. Field note: the awkward enterprise ritual.
2. Empathy: why time recording matters, and why the UI hurts.
3. Evidence: what the DOM and workflow reveal.
4. First AI interaction: critique a real slice, not a whole system.
5. Codex moment: annotate what is bad and turn it into a bounded change.
6. Feature rungs: userscript, templates, UI/holidays, harness, extension,
   API, MCP, each justified by a real person and a new proof boundary.
7. Governance: confirmations, audit trails, and human judgement.
8. Closing: the future is not "AI does admin". The future is that someone finally
   designs the integration layer instead of improvising it.

## Tone

Warm, mischievous, practical, with a streak of gallows humour. The joke is never
"people are bad at admin". The joke is that serious organisations still ask clever
humans to perform tiny weekly ceremonies of data entry that a computer could at
least prepare for review, and that we have all quietly agreed to find this normal.

Lines worth keeping:

- "The enemy is not time recording. The enemy is making the human act as the
  integration layer."
- "The agent did what we asked. That was the problem."
- "A browser agent is a lot of confidence wrapped around a mouse cursor."
- "Normal enterprise software. That is the disturbing part."
- "The first useful button."
- "Find the hidden integration work."

Avoid making the first segment sound like casual experimentation. The stronger
framing is fieldwork: observe the ritual, name the hidden policy, and only then
choose whether AI should critique, draft, test, or stay out of the way.

## Why this shape works for sceptics

- It starts with a familiar pain rather than abstract AI optimism.
- It shows restraint before it shows off any capability.
- Every demo stays inspectable, and you can undo it.
- Governance is part of the craft here, not a slide bolted on at the end.
